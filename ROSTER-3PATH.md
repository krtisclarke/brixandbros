# Brix and Bros — Full Roster (3-Path Upgrade Redesign)

Every Bro now has **three upgrade paths**. The rule of two:

> **You may put studs into only two of the three paths.** The moment you buy into a
> second path, the third locks for the rest of the battle. One of your two chosen
> paths may run all the way to its **capstone** (tier 3); the other stops at tier 2 —
> the same five purchases a Bro supports today, so nothing about the economy moves.

Tiers 1 and 2 sharpen the numbers. **Every capstone changes how the Bro plays.**

## Design Notes

**Why three paths, choose two.** Two paths ask one question: which one do you cap?
Three paths with the rule of two ask three: which pair, which capstone, and which
Bro on the board answers a different question than the one beside it. Each tower
goes from 2 real builds to 6 — pick a pair (3 ways), pick which of the pair caps (×2) —
without a single new purchase slot: the five-buy ceiling and every cost band is
unchanged, so the studs economy doesn't move.

**The overlap purge.** Before, nine towers bought "sees stealth" as a tier-1 filler,
and most paths were the same +damage/+speed ladder wearing different hats. Detection
is now a *choice* with an owner — but every class keeps exactly one cheap stage-one
answer, so no class is ever blind: Frostline's Keen Eyes (🔩90, the cheapest upgrade
in the game), Navy's Night Scope and Thermal Visor, the Shadow Diver born with it for
Mystic, and Support's Watchtower plus the Sonar Station itself. Scout Tilly still
lends her eyes to Frostline as a luxury, not a requirement. Nine copies down to a
deliberate handful — skip them all, and stealth is genuinely scary again.

**Capstones change behaviour, not numbers.** Every third tier now does something the
tower could not do at all before — ricochets, freeze meters, icy wakes, drift mines,
strafing dives, anchored storms, decoys, corrupt ground, an alarm dome. Tiers 1–2
stay numerical on purpose: cheap purchases should be easy to evaluate mid-wave, and
the identity-defining decision should sit at the top where it's saved up for.

**Roles were preserved, not reshuffled.** The Pebble Flinger still flings bricks in
all three futures; the paths disagree about *which pebble problem* she solves. Support
capstones were adapted to this game's actual rules — Bros never take damage here,
so the Fort's "defensive anchor" became a breach-triggered freeze dome rather than
a shield that nothing would ever spend.

**Heroes were left alone**, except two one-line class passives where the synergy was
already sitting there: Scout Tilly shares her eyes with Frostline (which is what a
scout is *for*), and Warden Kell's Corrosion now counts as a mystic curse so the new
Conduit and Plague Bearer paths have something to amplify. No growth curves, prices
or abilities moved.

**What's new under the hood — and what deliberately isn't.** Every one of the 180
upgrades was audited against the engine as it exists. 149 run on machinery already in
the game — the fx system (slow, stun, rot, armor shred), splash, pierce, chains,
volleys, auras, spikes and the income hooks. The rest run on a small set of shared
systems, each built once and reused, tagged inline where they appear:

- **Ground zones** *(10 capstones)* — craters, slicks, wakes, aurora fire, corrupt
  stains, squalls, quagmires. One system: a patch on the track with a lifetime and an
  effect, checked against vacuums the same way spike piles already are.
- **Vulnerability marks** *(6 capstones)* — "takes +N% damage from every source."
  One new field on a vacuum and one multiply where damage is dealt.
- **Knockback** *(3 capstones)* — a vacuum's progress is a distance along the
  track; knockback subtracts from it. It has simply never been used before.
- **The freeze meter** *(1 capstone)* — a hit counter on the victim. The Snowball
  Roller keeps it because "the third hit freezes" was the whole fantasy.
- **Repurposed spike piles** *(2 capstones)* — the Drift Mines and the ice decoy are
  the Ice Wall Builder's pile system doing new tricks: exploding on contact, or
  holding a vacuum instead of hurting it.

Two one-line additions ride along: a crit roll (Killing Frost — the game's first
random damage, deliberate and capstone-priced) and a ramp counter (Supernova Focus,
capped at +15 so deep-endless numbers cannot run away). Everything else that sounded
grander than the engine — bricks that "skip," beams that scorch lines nothing walks
on, decoys vacuums would have to *attack* in a game where vacuums only march —
was rewritten to promise exactly what the player will see.

**What this means for the flyout panel.** The selection panel shows two full paths
today; it will need to show three, with the locked path visibly greyed once two are
chosen, a "locked — two paths chosen" note in its place, and the same side-by-side
compression on short screens extended to three columns. That's an implementation
task alongside the data change, not a design decision — the panel was rebuilt
recently precisely so it could absorb this.
